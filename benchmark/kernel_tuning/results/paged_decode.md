# Paged decode / verify / prefill attention (PD1)

Receipts for the FlashInfer paged-attention dispatch on RTX 5090, RTX PRO 6000 and GB10.

## Method

All timings come from `benchmark/kernel_tuning/paged_attention_sweep.py`:
- Every timed graph replays many calls, so the ~1-2 us graph-replay floor is amortized.
- **hot** = one layer's Q/K/V buffers called 16 times.
- **engine** = consecutive layers, each with its own K/V, spanning >= 3x L2 (2-64 layers). This is the decode regime of the engine. All verdict tables below use it.
- Variants of one row (ours vs the provider plan) are timed in one process, in rotated order.

FlashInfer versions cannot share a process, so each version runs in its own worker. The verdict runs (sections 3-4) control for cross-process bias:
- Each round starts a fresh worker per environment, in an order shuffled per round. Every worker first allocates a random-size device pad (0-1 GiB), so buffer placement differs from round to round.
- **0.6.11 twin (A/A):** a second FlashInfer 0.6.11.post3 worker per round. Its ratio to the first measures what the method can resolve.
  - Most half-widths are 0.1-1%; GB10 is wider, up to 4.7% on short prefill.
  - 6 of the 297 A/A intervals in section 3 exclude 1, about the 5% a 95% interval allows. Five are within 0.5% of 1: PRO decode B2/KV16384, GB10 decode B4/KV512, GB10 Qwen3.5 B3/KV1024, 5090 verify B1/KV2048 and 5090 prefill B4/Q512.
  - The sixth is GB10 prefill B1/Q22, at 1.055 [1.009, 1.103]. That is as large as the row's 0.7.0/0.6.11 ratio, so the 6-round run does not attribute that row; a 16-round rerun does (section 3, under the GB10 prefill table).
- **vLLM environment control:** on the RTX 5090, vLLM's FA2 kernel was also timed inside the 0.7.0 worker. It runs at 0.996-1.007x of the same kernel in the 0.6.11 worker, so the 0.7.0 environment itself does not slow kernels down.
- Ratios are means over 6 paired rounds (log scale), with 95% t intervals. A ratio is called slower when the whole interval lies above 1.
- FlashInfer 0.7.0.post1 is installed side by side per the engine recipe (cuda-python 13.3.1, cuda-core 1.0.1, nccl4py 0.6.0, nccl-extensions 0.1.0, all `--no-deps`). GB10 runs are pinned to the X925 cores.

Comparators:
- **vLLM 0.31.0**: its sm12x decode dispatch, the vllm-flash-attn FA2 `varlen_fwd` with block size 16, paired in-process with ours under 0.6.11.
- **SGLang 72730d6**: its graph decode wrapper arguments (tensor cores for GQA >= 4, page size 1, no fixed split) on FlashInfer 0.7.1, SGLang's pin.

## 1. The B=4, KV=8192 decode cliff: root cause

This is L2 residency in a single-layer, hot-replay microbenchmark, not a kernel or schedule defect.
- The FlashInfer plan, CTA count and kernel variant are the same at B=3 and B=4.
- One layer's K/V at B=3, KV=8192 is 96 MiB, which fits the RTX 5090's 96 MiB L2 when replayed hot. At B=4 it is 128 MiB, which does not, so B=4 runs at DRAM speed (~1.5 TB/s).
- B=2, KV=16384 (also 128 MiB) shows the same step.
- In the engine regime every layer reads its own cold K/V. There the curve is smooth and monotone in B and KV on all three devices; for example the 5090 at KV=8192 takes 27/47/68/88 us for B=1-4.
- The PRO 6000 (128 MiB L2) and GB10 (24 MiB) show the same step at their own L2 sizes.
- No code change was needed for the cliff. Section 2's dispatch changes do not affect it.

## 2. Dispatch (`FlashInferPagedAttention(dispatch="device")`, FixedQuery tiers)

**Graph decode:**
- Qwen3-8B heads (32/8/128) use FlashInfer's CUDA-core decode kernel at B=2-4 on the RTX 5090 and RTX PRO 6000.
- **GB10 keeps tensor cores at every batch**, SGLang 72730d6's choice. CUDA cores were 3-12% faster there at KV <= 2048, but 1-2% slower than SGLang at KV >= 4096 (B2/KV8192: 299.1 vs 295.2 us). A captured graph cannot switch kernels with KV length, so the row-wise market rule decides.
- The tensor-core path uses per-device Split-KV tables (splits per request by B x KV bucket) through `fixed_split_size`.
- **GB10 Qwen3.5 (16/4/256) at KV >= 8192:** S = 8 at B1, 4 at B2 and 2 at B3-4 (section 5.1). These rows ship under the GB10 rule against the provider plan, not the 2%-under-both-versions bar of the sm_120 tables. The split is chosen on the host at plan time from the construction-time batch, the device and the KV bucket; the captured layout check still applies.
- A graph keeps the provider capture plan, and a table plan must reproduce that plan's layout (checked on the host). Reading the native plan's capacity and layout lives in `_flashinfer.py` and requires a qualified FlashInfer (0.6.11.post3 or 0.7.0.post1, both the distribution and the imported module); otherwise the provider default stays.

**Short fresh causal prefill (page size 1, hd128):**
- Each prompt is planned as pseudo-requests of c queries, with c set per device from the total query count. FlashInfer's bottom-right causal mask makes this exact.
- It applies only when every request is fresh (KV = Q) and the borrowed workspaces fit.

**Workspace sizing:** `FlashInferPagedAttention.workspace_size()` defaults to `dispatch="device"` and covers every route the dispatcher can take within the stated bounds: the CUDA-core decode route and the prefill pseudo-requests. With exactly the advertised sizes, the measured route is taken (GPU test `test_exact_workspace_routes_cuda_core_graph_decode`, prefill assertions in `check_prefill`). `dispatch="provider"` sizes the provider route alone. (The earlier bench enlarged the workspace by hand; prefill rows that needed more pseudo-requests than the old sizing fell back to the provider plan, e.g. RTX PRO 6000 B4/Q22.)

**FixedQuery hd128 sm_120:**
- Measured on kernels/paged-decode (rc-0.1.12 base): minimum-chunk tiers (32 tokens up to 512 keys) and (64 tokens up to 768 keys). The rc-0.1.13 branch `kernels/paged-decode-rc` does not carry them: rc-0.1.13 keeps the native hd128 minimum because chunk 32 flipped a near-tie greedy token in Qwen3-8B DFlash parity (a343142). Its verify rows at 512-768 keys are the `m128` columns of section 3.

The dispatch is the same under both FlashInfer versions. Screening under 0.7.0.post1 (every decode/verify/prefill variant of the sweep, sections 3-4) found no 0.7-only choice that beats our 0.6.11 dispatch on a regressed row. The candidates were CUDA-core vs tensor-core decode, fixed splits of 64-512 pages, verify minimum chunks 16-128, and prefill chunks 4/8/16 or halves, with and without Split-KV. Section 4 explains why.

What changed in FlashInfer 0.7.0.post1 (source comparison of the two wheels):
- **FA2 paged prefill kernel** (used for prefill, verify and tensor-core decode): the kernel body was restructured around a split-D/VO-split output loop. It adds a block barrier before `griddepcontrol.launch_dependents`, plus a `SharedStoragePaged` layout and `SAME_KV_STRIDES` template.
  - **RTX PRO 6000:** the kernel itself is 0.2 us slower for tiny prefill (4.03 -> 4.22-4.26 us, Q 8-64).
  - **RTX 5090:** the kernel is 0.06-0.16 us slower, and the PDL overlap between consecutive attention calls drops from 0.15-0.17 to 0.02-0.12 us.
  - Long verify on both devices: +2-3% (B1/KV4096: 15.70 -> 16.08 us on the 5090, 17.04 -> 17.54 us on the PRO).
- **CUDA-core decode kernel:** the online-softmax update now clamps the running maximum (`max(st.m, -FLT_MAX)`) on every tile. This fixes NaN for fully masked tiles, but adds a dependent instruction to the latency-bound loop. B2/KV512: 7.17 -> 7.38 us (5090), 8.32 -> 8.61 us (PRO).
- The merge kernel is unchanged or faster under 0.7 (hd256: 2.05 -> 1.79 us), which is why some Qwen3.5 rows are at parity.

No dispatch choice among FlashInfer's kernels avoids these costs. Every FlashInfer attention kernel ours or the provider can pick for these rows is one of the two regressed kernels. Recovering them needs either an upstream FlashInfer change or our own CuTe DSL kernels for these shapes.

- **CUDA-core decode at B=1 on sm_120:** -6-15% at KV 1-8K, but +11-17% at KV 512. A graph is captured per batch, so the kernel cannot switch with KV.
- **CUDA-core decode on GB10:** -3-12% at KV <= 2048, +1-2% vs SGLang at KV >= 4096. Not shipped under the row-wise market rule (section 2).
- **Qwen3.5 hd256 CUDA-core decode under 0.7.0:** much faster than under 0.6.11 (KV512: 12.7 vs 21.4 us on the 5090). At B2-4 it beats tensor cores by 5-13% at KV 1-4K, but it loses 13-60% at KV 512 (and at B1 up to KV 2048). Same per-batch limitation.
- **Verify minimum chunk 32-64 at 1040 keys on sm_120:** -5-21% at B1-3 on the RTX 5090, but PRO B4 loses. It would need batch-aware tiers in the rc-0.1.12 table.
- **Prefill query split beyond 256 total queries:** chunks of 4-16 give 0.57-0.83x at B2/Q256 and B4/Q256 on the RTX 5090. Not part of PD1's rows.
- **`enable_pdl=False` on graph decode:** wins only for back-to-back attention. In the engine the preceding KV store triggers dependents early, so this needs an engine-level A/B.
